How to Choose a Qualified Public Adjuster in St. Louis: 5 Key Questions to Ask

June 20, 2026

When you're facing a significant insurance claim for property damage in St. Louis, Missouri, or Illinois, hiring a public adjuster can make a substantial difference in your settlement outcome. However, not all public adjusters are created equal. Choosing the right representative requires careful consideration and asking the right questions. This guide will help you navigate the selection process and ensure you partner with a qualified professional who will advocate for your best interests.

What Is a Public Adjuster?

A public adjuster is a licensed insurance professional who works exclusively for policyholders, not insurance companies. They handle every aspect of the claims process, from documenting damage and preparing estimates to negotiating with insurance adjusters and ensuring you receive a fair settlement. Unlike company adjusters who represent the insurer's interests, public adjusters are legally obligated to act in your best interest.

In Missouri and Illinois, public adjusters must be licensed by the state insurance department and adhere to strict ethical standards. They typically charge a percentage of the final settlement, which aligns their interests with yours—you don't pay unless they recover money for you.

5 Key Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Public Adjuster

1. Are You Licensed and Bonded in Missouri and/or Illinois?

This is the most fundamental question. Public adjusting is regulated at the state level, and adjusters must hold valid licenses for the states where they operate.

  • Missouri: Verify license through the Missouri Department of Insurance
  • Illinois: Check license status with the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation
  • Bonding: Reputable adjusters carry a surety bond as required by state law
  • Verification: Ask for their license number and verify it yourself online

Never hire an adjuster who cannot provide proof of current licensing in your state. Working with an unlicensed adjuster is illegal and leaves you with no recourse if something goes wrong.

2. What Is Your Experience with Claims Like Mine?

Experience matters, especially with specific types of damage. Ask about their track record with claims similar to yours:

  • Property type: Residential, commercial, or multifamily?
  • Damage cause: Water, fire, hail, wind, theft, vandalism, or other?
  • Claim size: Have they handled claims of similar value?
  • Local knowledge: Do they understand St. Louis-specific building codes and insurance practices?

A qualified adjuster should be able to share examples of past successes (without violating client confidentiality) and explain how their experience applies to your situation.

3. How Do You Charge for Your Services?

Understanding the fee structure is crucial before signing any agreement:

  • Standard fee: Most public adjusters charge a percentage of the final settlement (typically 5-15%, depending on claim size and complexity)
  • No upfront costs: Reputable adjusters work on contingency—you pay only if they recover money
  • Fee cap: Missouri and Illinois may limit the percentage adjusters can charge
  • Additional expenses: Clarify if you're responsible for any out-of-pocket expenses (like expert reports or contractor fees)
  • Written agreement: Insist on a clear, written contract detailing all fees and terms

Be wary of adjusters who demand large upfront payments or whose fees seem significantly outside the normal range.

4. Can You Provide References from Past Clients?

A track record of satisfied clients is one of the best indicators of quality service:

  • Recent references: Ask for references from clients with similar claims in the last 12-24 months
  • Specific outcomes: Ask about settlement amounts achieved and client satisfaction
  • Online reviews: Check Google, BBB, and other review platforms for additional feedback
  • Professional affiliations: Membership in organizations like NAPIA (National Association of Public Insurance Adjusters) indicates commitment to professional standards

If an adjuster hesitates to provide references or has predominantly negative reviews, consider it a serious warning sign.

5. What Is Your Process for Handling a Claim from Start to Finish?

Understanding their workflow helps you set expectations and gauge their thoroughness:

  • Initial inspection: Do they conduct a comprehensive damage assessment?
  • Documentation: How do they gather evidence (photos, videos, inventories, expert reports)?
  • Estimate preparation: Do they use industry-standard estimating software?
  • Communication: How often will you receive updates, and through what channels?
  • Negotiation: What is their approach to settling with the insurance company?
  • Closing: How do they ensure all aspects of the claim are resolved before considering it complete?

A professional adjuster will have a clear, systematic process and be able to explain each step in detail.

How to Verify a Public Adjuster's Credentials

Beyond asking questions, take these steps to verify an adjuster's qualifications:

  • License lookup: Use state insurance department websites to confirm license status and check for disciplinary actions
  • Bond verification: Confirm their surety bond is current through the state licensing portal
  • Insurance: Ensure they carry liability and errors-and-omissions insurance
  • BBB rating: Check their Better Business Bureau rating and complaint history
  • References: Contact past clients directly to ask about their experience

Red Flags to Watch Out For

Be cautious if you encounter any of these warning signs:

  • Pressure to sign immediately: Reputable adjusters give you time to review agreements and make informed decisions
  • Guarantees of specific settlement amounts: No ethical adjuster can guarantee a particular outcome
  • Refusal to provide references: Transparency about past performance is standard practice
  • Unclear fee structure: Vagueness about costs or reluctance to provide a written contract
  • Lack of local presence: Adjusters who don't have a physical office or local knowledge may not understand regional nuances
  • Poor communication: Difficulty reaching them or slow responses during initial conversations

Why Local Expertise Matters in St. Louis

Hiring a public adjuster with specific St. Louis area experience offers distinct advantages:

  • Building code knowledge: Understanding St. Louis County and municipal code requirements for repairs
  • Contractor relationships: Established connections with reputable local contractors for accurate estimates
  • Weather patterns: Familiarity with common storm types and damage patterns in the region
  • Insurance company practices: Experience with how specific insurers handle claims in Missouri and Illinois markets
  • Accessibility: Ability to visit your property quickly for inspections and meetings

Local adjusters understand the unique challenges of our area, from the clay soil that affects foundation repairs to the specific hail and wind patterns that impact roofs.

When to Hire a Public Adjuster

Consider professional representation if:

  • Your claim involves significant damage (typically over $10,000)
  • You're dealing with a complex loss (multiple types of damage or policy interpretations)
  • The insurance company has denied or underpaid your claim
  • You don't have the time or expertise to manage the claims process effectively
  • You feel overwhelmed or uncertain about how to proceed with your claim

Even for smaller claims, a consultation with a public adjuster can provide valuable insights into your policy coverage and claim options.

Key Takeaways

  • Verify licensing: Always confirm an adjuster's license is current in your state
  • Ask about experience: Ensure they have handled claims similar to yours in type and size
  • Understand fees: Know exactly how they charge and get everything in writing
  • Check references: Talk to past clients about their experience and outcomes
  • Understand their process: A clear, systematic approach indicates professionalism
  • Trust your instincts: Choose someone you feel comfortable with and who communicates clearly

Selecting the right public adjuster is one of the most important decisions you'll make during the insurance claims process. By asking these five key questions and doing your due diligence, you'll be well-equipped to choose a qualified professional who will advocate for your best interests and help you achieve a fair settlement.
