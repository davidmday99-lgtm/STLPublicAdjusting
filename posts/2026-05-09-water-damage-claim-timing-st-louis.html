When to File a Water Damage Claim: Timing, Documentation, and Next Steps

May 9, 2026

A pipe burst in your St. Louis home at 2 AM. Water is flooding your basement. Your first instinct is panic—but your second thought should be: When do I file this claim?

Timing matters in water damage claims. File too late, and you risk losing coverage or having the insurer argue you didn't mitigate loss. File without proper documentation, and you'll struggle to prove the loss amount. Act immediately with the right steps, and you maximize your recovery.

This guide covers when to file a water damage claim in St. Louis and Missouri, what triggers coverage, documentation timing, and how to navigate the first 48 hours after water damage occurs.

Immediate Actions: The First 24 Hours After Water Damage

Hour 1-2: Stop the Water, Ensure Safety

Your first priority is not the claim—it's stopping the damage.

If water is actively flowing:

  • Locate and shut off the water main (usually near the street or basement). Know where it is before an emergency. If you can't find it, call a plumber immediately.
  • Turn off electricity to affected areas if water is near outlets, appliances, or electrical panels. Safety first.
  • Move valuables and furniture to dry areas to prevent additional loss.
  • Open windows and doors to increase air flow and begin drying (weather permitting).
  • Do not enter flooded areas if you suspect electrical hazard or contamination.

This is your "duty to mitigate." Insurance policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further loss. Acting immediately to stop water flow and protect property demonstrates you're meeting this obligation—and protects your claim if the insurer later argues you were negligent.

Hour 2-4: Document the Scene (Before Cleanup)

Do not start cleanup or removal until you've documented the damage.

Take photos and video:

  • Wide shots: Show the full scope of water damage—flooded rooms, affected areas, extent of spread.
  • Close-ups: Document specific damage to flooring, walls, carpets, furniture, appliances.
  • Water level marks: If water has receded, show marks on walls or furniture indicating height.
  • Point of origin: Photograph where the water came from—the burst pipe, failed seal, overflowing toilet, etc.
  • Damaged items: Photo every item water touched (furniture, electronics, boxes, belongings).
  • Timestamp your photos: Most phones automatically tag photos with date/time. Keep this metadata intact (don't edit or compress excessively).

Store photos in multiple places: your phone, email, cloud storage. If your phone gets damaged or lost, you have backups.

Hour 4-6: Call Your Insurance Company

Report the loss as soon as possible—ideally within 24 hours of discovering the damage.

When you call:

  • Have your policy number ready
  • Provide a brief description of what happened (pipe burst, roof leak, overflowing appliance, etc.)
  • Explain when you discovered the damage
  • Ask for the claim number and adjuster contact information
  • Ask about deadlines for documentation submission
  • Request written confirmation via email

Pro tip: Most insurers have 24-hour claims hotlines. Call immediately, even at night. Early reporting shows diligence and starts the clock on the insurer's investigation timeline.

Hour 6-24: Mitigation vs. Cleanup

This is critical: There's a difference between mitigation and cleanup.

Mitigation (covered): Actions to prevent further loss—stopping the water, removing saturated materials to prevent mold spread, running fans and dehumidifiers, placing wet items in sunlight.

Cleanup (varies): Removing damaged items, demolition, restoration. Some insurers cover cleanup costs; others don't. Always ask first.

Rule: Don't throw anything away without photographing it and asking your adjuster if they want to see it first. Premature disposal can trigger disputes about what was actually damaged and the loss amount.

When to File: Legal Deadlines and Policy Timeframes

Missouri's Water Damage Claim Deadlines

Missouri law (Section 379.235, RSMo) requires:

  • Report losses within 60 days of discovery (or within one year, whichever comes first). Missing this deadline can result in claim denial.
  • Provide proof of loss within 90 days. This is a written itemization of damages—what was damaged and estimated repair/replacement cost.

Most policies are more restrictive. They typically require:

  • Immediate notice of the loss (24-48 hours)
  • Proof of loss within 30-60 days (depending on policy language)

Bottom line: Don't wait. Report within 24 hours of discovering damage, and provide documentation within 30-45 days.

What "Water Damage" Means for Coverage Decisions

Here's where timing intersects with coverage: When did the water start? How long did it flow?

Homeowners policies distinguish between:

  • Sudden water damage (covered): Sudden burst pipe, sudden roof leak from storm, sudden appliance failure. File immediately.
  • Gradual seepage (often denied): Slow roof leak that's been ongoing for months. Insurers claim you had time to notice and repair but didn't. This gets denied as a maintenance issue.
  • Flood (excluded): Water from outside sources (rain, groundwater, river/stream overflow). Not covered unless you have separate flood insurance.

Timing matters because it determines coverage classification. If you report a burst pipe 2 hours after discovering water, it's clearly sudden. If you report water damage 6 months after first noticing stains, the insurer may deny it as gradual deterioration you failed to address.

Documentation Checklist: What to Provide When

Your adjuster will request documentation over the first few weeks. Have this ready:

Within 24 Hours of Report

  • Photos and video from the damage scene
  • Brief written description of what happened (date, time, cause)
  • Any evidence of the trigger event (photo of burst pipe, maintenance records, weather report for storm)

Within 7 Days of Report

  • List of damaged items: Go room by room and document what water touched (furniture, electronics, clothing, boxes, valuables)
  • Original purchase receipts: For items you want to claim, provide proof of original cost. Credit card statements, warranty paperwork, store receipts all work.
  • Photos of each damaged item: Close-ups with context (showing the item in the room, not just isolated)
  • Proof you're not a slob: While it shouldn't matter, photos showing your home was normally well-maintained help counter "pre-existing poor conditions" arguments

Within 30 Days of Report

  • Restoration/repair estimates: Get written quotes from licensed contractors for drying, mold remediation, repairs, or replacement
  • Proof of loss form: This is an itemized, detailed list of losses with estimated replacement cost. Your adjuster will provide a template, or you can create one with contractor estimates
  • Explanation of cause: If the water damage resulted from something controllable (you left a window open and it rained, a hose came loose from a washing machine), document this with photos or the maintenance record

Ongoing (As Adjuster Requests)

  • Receipts for mitigation costs (dehumidifiers, fans, tarping)—often covered
  • Communication with contractors or plumbers
  • Any additional photos or documentation requested

Real-World Scenario: Timing a Water Damage Claim Correctly

The Situation: A St. Louis couple's washing machine hose failed on a Saturday morning in April. Water flooded their laundry room and seeped into adjacent hallway and bedroom closet. They discovered it at 8 AM.

Their Timeline:

  • 8:00 AM: Discover water. Shut off water main. Turn off electricity to laundry room. Take 30+ photos of wet carpet, walls, ruined boxes in closet. Video the damage.
  • 9:00 AM: Call insurance company. Report loss. Get claim number and adjuster assignment.
  • 10:00 AM: Call restoration company for emergency mitigation. Have them come the same day to start water removal and drying.
  • Afternoon: Create itemized list of damaged items (boxes of holiday decorations, shoes, clothes, small furniture). Take close-up photos of each.
  • Monday (2 days later): Meet with adjuster for inspection. Show photos. Provide initial damage list.
  • Within 5 days: Get restoration company's quote for water extraction, dehumidification, carpet replacement.
  • Within 20 days: Submit detailed proof of loss with all photos, receipts for damaged items, and restoration estimates.
  • Within 30 days: Adjuster approves claim. Settlement issued minus deductible.

Result: Claim approved and paid within 30 days because they reported immediately, documented thoroughly, and provided comprehensive estimates promptly.

What Delays Water Damage Claims

Delays happen when:

  • Late reporting: You wait a week or more to notify the insurer. This delays adjuster assignment and inspection.
  • Missing documentation: You can't provide receipts or original cost proof for items you want to claim. The adjuster has to estimate value, which often results in lower settlements.
  • Incomplete damage description: You report the loss vaguely ("water damage in basement") without specifics. The adjuster has to request clarification, adding days to the process.
  • Premature cleanup: You threw away damaged items before the adjuster photographed them. Now the insurer questions whether damage was as extensive as you claim.
  • Contractor delays: You're waiting for bids or restoration companies are backlogged (common during heavy storms). Provide estimates promptly even if restoration hasn't started.
  • Disputed coverage: The insurer argues the damage was from gradual seepage or flood (excluded). This requires investigation and may go to appeal.

Special Situations: When to File Immediately

Mold Risk

Water damage that sits increases mold risk. File immediately if:

  • Water saturation is extensive and drying will take days
  • Affected areas are in dark or low-ventilation zones (basements, crawlspaces, closets)
  • You see visible mold growth within 48 hours

Mold remediation costs add up fast and require professional assessment. Early filing lets the adjuster determine coverage for mold prevention and remediation.

Structural Damage

If water has potentially compromised structure (wood rot in beams, water in walls, foundation saturation), file and request urgent inspection. Extended water damage to structural elements can be catastrophic.

Multiple Rooms Affected

Water that spreads to multiple rooms is a larger claim. File immediately and document the full extent. The adjuster will want to inspect before you begin any cleanup or mitigation.

After the Adjuster Inspects: Next Steps

What to Expect During the Inspection

The adjuster will:

  • Walk through affected areas and document damage
  • Ask questions about cause and timeline
  • Identify what's covered and what's not
  • Request any missing documentation
  • Take their own photos

Your role: Be present. Show the adjuster photos you took. Point out damage they might miss. Answer questions honestly. Don't overstate or understate losses.

If Coverage is Disputed

If the adjuster initially denies coverage or reduces the estimated loss, don't panic. Request detailed written explanation. Ask why specific items aren't covered. If you disagree, appeal (see our post on Insurance Claim Denial Appeal for the full process).

Common disputes in water damage: Adjuster claims the water came from outside (flood, excluded) when you believe it came from inside (burst pipe, covered). Get a water damage assessment from a professional to determine the source.

Settlement and Payment

Once the adjuster approves the claim:

  • Deductible is applied (typically $500-$1,000, sometimes $2,500)
  • Settlement amount is the approved damages minus deductible
  • Check is issued, usually within 5-10 business days
  • You can request payment to you and the lienholder (if you have a mortgage), or just to you

Should You File a Water Damage Claim? When It Makes Sense

Not every water incident requires an insurance claim. Consider:

  • Estimated repair cost vs. deductible: If damage is $600 and your deductible is $1,000, filing doesn't make financial sense.
  • Claim history: Multiple claims in a short period can increase premiums or risk non-renewal. One significant claim is usually worth it; multiple small claims may not be.
  • Coverage uncertainty: If you're unsure if the damage is covered, ask your adjuster before formally opening a claim.

For significant damage (over $2,000-$3,000), filing almost always makes sense. The potential payout outweighs any premium impact.

Why Timing and Documentation Matter

Insurers process thousands of claims. The ones that move fastest and settle highest are the ones with:

  • Immediate reporting (24-48 hours)
  • Clear, comprehensive documentation
  • Professional estimates and proof of loss
  • Transparency about cause and timeline

The ones that drag on or result in disputes are delayed reports, missing documentation, vague descriptions, and cleanup before adjuster approval.
