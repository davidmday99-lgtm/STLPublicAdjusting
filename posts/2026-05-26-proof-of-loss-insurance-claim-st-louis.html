How to Complete a Proof of Loss for Your Insurance Claim in Missouri

May 26, 2026

You've filed an insurance claim in Missouri after property damage. Your adjuster has completed their inspection. Now your insurance company sends a form titled "Proof of Loss" and asks you to complete it. Many homeowners and business owners in St. Louis and Illinois don't realize how critical this single document is—or how a poorly completed proof of loss can delay, reduce, or even result in claim denial.

The proof of loss is not just paperwork. It's a legal document that formally establishes what you're claiming was damaged and how much those damages cost. Insurance companies use it to validate your claim and determine their liability. Completing it correctly protects your rights and strengthens your settlement position.

What Is a Proof of Loss?

A proof of loss is a legal statement. You're certifying (under oath or affirmation) that you've suffered a loss covered by your insurance policy. The document typically requires you to:

  • Describe the incident that caused the damage (hail storm, water damage, fire, etc.)
  • List all items or areas damaged
  • Provide the cost to repair or replace each item
  • Explain the cause and date of loss
  • Attest that the information is accurate and truthful

Insurance companies use the proof of loss as evidence of the claim's validity. If information in your proof of loss contradicts what you told the adjuster, or if your estimates seem unreasonably high or low, the insurer may question your claim's credibility.

When Is a Proof of Loss Due?

Missouri insurance law requires you to submit a proof of loss within specific timeframes. According to Mo. Rev. Stat. § 375.936, most homeowners policies require proof of loss to be filed within:

  • 60 days of the loss (this is the most common deadline)
  • Longer periods (90–120 days) in some policies for complex claims involving multiple damage locations
  • Your policy may specify a different deadline—check your declarations page

If you miss this deadline, your insurer can deny your claim. Some insurers will request an extension, but don't count on it. File your proof of loss promptly.

Pro tip: Don't wait for the insurance company to send the form. After your adjuster's inspection, send a written request for the proof of loss form immediately. This starts the clock and gives you time to gather documentation without rushing.

What Should You Include in Your Proof of Loss?

A complete proof of loss needs detail. Here's what to include for each damaged area or item:

Property Information and Loss Details

  • Your name, policy number, and contact information
  • Date and time of the loss
  • Cause of loss (named storm, hail, fire, water damage, etc.)
  • Brief description of how the damage occurred
  • Weather conditions (if relevant—hail size, wind speeds)

Itemized Damage List

Don't just write "roof damage—$20,000." Break it down:

  • Location: "Master bedroom roof, south slope" or "Kitchen ceiling"
  • Item damaged: "Asphalt shingles," "drywall," "hardwood flooring"
  • Extent of damage: "Approximately 800 sq ft of roof with hail-induced granule loss and soft spots" or "Water damage to 60% of kitchen ceiling with mold risk"
  • Repair/replacement cost: From contractor estimates (include those estimates with your proof of loss)
  • Date observed: When you first noticed the damage

Supporting Documentation

Your proof of loss should be accompanied by:

  • Detailed photos and video of all damage
  • Written repair estimates from licensed contractors
  • Receipts for emergency repairs or temporary measures (tarping, cleanup)
  • Proof of ownership for damaged personal property
  • Any expert assessments (structural engineer report, environmental consultant, etc.)
  • Documentation of secondary damage (water damage to interior, mold, etc.)

Common Mistakes to Avoid When Filing Proof of Loss

Mistake #1: Underestimating Damage

Some homeowners list only obvious damage and miss hidden or secondary damage. After property damage in St. Louis, comprehensive documentation is critical. Water damage, mold risk, and structural issues often emerge after the initial inspection. Include these in your proof of loss based on contractor assessments.

Mistake #2: Providing Incomplete Estimates

Vague cost estimates hurt your credibility. Use detailed contractor quotes that itemize labor, materials, permits, and waste removal. The more specific, the harder it is for the insurer to dispute your numbers.

Mistake #3: Filing Without Contractor Documentation

Never file a proof of loss with only your own cost estimates. Licensed contractor quotes carry weight with insurance companies. If your estimates significantly exceed the insurer's initial assessment, contractor quotes explain the difference.

Mistake #4: Inconsistency With What You Told the Adjuster

If you told the adjuster "the storm hit on May 20" but your proof of loss says "May 19," the insurer will question your reliability. Coordinate with your documentation carefully. Review what you said during the inspection before filing.

Mistake #5: Missing the Deadline

The 60-day deadline is firm in most Missouri policies. Calendar it. Set a reminder. An insurer can legally deny a claim simply because proof of loss wasn't filed on time, regardless of whether the damage is legitimate.

How to Structure Your Proof of Loss Document

Format matters. Use a clear, professional structure:

  • Header: Policy number, claim number, date filed
  • Loss summary: One paragraph describing the incident
  • Itemized damage: Organized by location (roof, siding, interior, etc.) or by room
  • Cost summary: Total damage amount and itemized breakdown
  • Attachments: Reference the contractor quotes, photos, and supporting documents
  • Certification: A statement that the information is true and accurate, signed and dated

Should You Include an Appraisal or Expert Assessment?

If you've had an independent appraisal, structural engineer report, or environmental assessment done (common for complex claims), include these in your proof of loss. They strengthen your position significantly, especially if the insurer's initial estimate differs from professional assessments.

What Happens After You File Your Proof of Loss?

Once you submit it, the insurance company has several options:

  • Accept it: The insurer agrees your documented damage is covered and will pay your claim
  • Request clarification: They ask for more documentation or explanation on specific items
  • Dispute it: They disagree with your damage assessment or claim values (this triggers appraisal if you can't agree)
  • Request amendments: They ask you to revise or correct information

Missouri law requires the insurer to acknowledge receipt of your proof of loss and provide written explanation if they deny any portion of your claim.

Key Takeaways: Completing Your Proof of Loss

  • File on time: Respect the 60-day deadline—missing it can result in claim denial regardless of merit
  • Be comprehensive: Document every area of damage, including hidden and secondary damage
  • Include contractor quotes: Professional estimates validate your claim values
  • Stay organized: Use a clear structure with itemized lists and supporting documentation
  • Maintain consistency: Ensure your proof of loss matches what you told the adjuster
  • Provide supporting evidence: Photos, videos, receipts, and expert assessments strengthen your position

Your proof of loss is the foundation of your insurance claim. Completed correctly, it accelerates payment and prevents disputes. Done poorly, it can trigger denial or significantly delay your settlement. Take time to document thoroughly and present professionally.
