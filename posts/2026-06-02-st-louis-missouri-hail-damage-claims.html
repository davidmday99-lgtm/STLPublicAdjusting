St. Louis Missouri Hail Damage Claims: What Homeowners Need to Know

June 2, 2026

Recent severe weather has brought significant hail damage to the St. Louis metropolitan area and throughout Missouri. If your home, vehicle, or property was damaged by hail, understanding the insurance claim process is critical to maximizing your recovery and avoiding costly mistakes.

Hail damage claims present unique challenges that differ from other property damage claims. The damage is often less visible than storm or fire damage, insurers may delay or deny claims based on "cosmetic damage" arguments, and the timeline for proper documentation is critical. This guide explains everything St. Louis and Missouri homeowners need to know about hail damage insurance claims.

Understanding Hail Damage Types

Hail damage affects different materials in distinct ways:

Roof Damage

  • Asphalt shingles: Granule loss, bruising, cracking, or punctures
  • Metal roofs: Dents, dimpling, or compromised seams
  • Tile roofs: Cracks, chips, or broken tiles
  • Flat roofs: Punctures, membrane damage, or compromised seams

Siding and Exterior Damage

  • Vinyl siding: Cracks, holes, or dislodged panels
  • Aluminum siding: Dents, dings, or punctures
  • Brick and masonry: Spalling, chipped mortar, or cracked bricks
  • Stucco: Cracks, chips, or compromised surface

Window and Glass Damage

  • Window glass: Cracks, chips, or complete breakage
  • Skylights: Cracked or broken glass panels
  • Storm windows: Damaged protective glazing

Missouri Hail Damage Claim Timeline

Missouri law provides specific timelines for hail damage claims. Understanding these deadlines is essential:

Reporting Your Claim: Immediate

  • File within 30 days: Missouri insurers typically expect prompt notice of damage
  • Document immediately: Take photos and videos before any temporary repairs
  • Secure your property: Make temporary repairs to prevent additional damage (keep receipts)

Proof of Loss: 60 Days

  • Standard deadline: Missouri requires proof of loss within 60 days of the loss
  • Professional documentation: Consider hiring a public adjuster or contractor for accurate damage assessment
  • Comprehensive documentation: Include photos, videos, contractor estimates, and repair receipts

Insurer Inspection: 10-15 Business Days

  • Initial inspection: Insurer should schedule within 10-15 business days
  • Be present: Always be present during the adjuster's inspection
  • Document everything: Take notes and photos during the inspection

Common Hail Damage Claim Mistakes

Avoid these costly errors that can result in claim denial or underpayment:

Mistake 1: Delaying Claim Filing

Waiting too long to file your claim can result in denial based on "late notice." Missouri courts generally require claims to be filed within a "reasonable time," which insurers typically interpret as 30-60 days.

Mistake 2: Inadequate Documentation

Hail damage is often subtle and requires proper documentation. Smartphone photos taken from ground level often miss critical roof damage. Professional drone photography or roofing contractor assessments provide much more comprehensive documentation.

Mistake 3: Accepting the First Settlement Offer

Insurers often provide low initial settlement offers based on incomplete damage assessment. Never accept the first offer without independent verification of the damage extent and repair costs.

Mistake 4: Making Permanent Repairs Before Documentation

While temporary repairs to prevent additional damage are necessary and should be documented with receipts, never make permanent repairs before the insurer has inspected the damage and you have proper documentation.

Missouri Cosmetic Damage Rule: Some insurers argue that hail damage is "cosmetic only" and doesn't affect functionality. Missouri courts have consistently ruled that hail damage that affects the appearance, value, or marketability of property is covered, even if functionality isn't immediately compromised.

Maximizing Your Hail Damage Recovery

Follow these strategies to ensure you receive full compensation for your hail damage:

Step 1: Document Everything Immediately

  • Take comprehensive photos and videos from multiple angles
  • Document all damaged areas, including less obvious locations
  • Save weather reports and hail size documentation for your area
  • Keep receipts for any temporary repairs or emergency expenses

Step 2: Get Professional Damage Assessment

  • Hire a qualified roofing contractor for roof damage assessment
  • Consider hiring a public adjuster to represent your interests
  • Obtain multiple contractor estimates for major damage
  • Use professional drone photography for comprehensive roof documentation

Step 3: Understand Your Policy Coverage

  • Review your policy's actual cash value (ACV) vs. replacement cost value (RCV) provisions
  • Understand your deductible and how it applies to hail claims
  • Check for any hail-specific exclusions or limitations
  • Verify coverage for additional living expenses if your home becomes uninhabitable

Step 4: Negotiate Effectively

  • Don't accept the first settlement offer without review
  • Provide detailed contractor estimates that justify your claim value
  • Reference Missouri case law on cosmetic damage coverage
  • Consider professional representation if the claim is complex or disputed

When to Hire a Public Adjuster

Consider hiring a public adjuster if:

  • Your claim involves significant damage (over $10,000)
  • The insurer is arguing "cosmetic damage only"
  • You're unsure about policy coverage or claim procedures
  • The insurer's settlement offer seems inadequate
  • You need help with documentation or contractor coordination

Public adjusters in Missouri work on a contingency basis (typically 10-15% of the final settlement) and only get paid if they increase your recovery. They understand Missouri insurance law and can ensure your claim meets all legal requirements while maximizing your compensation.
