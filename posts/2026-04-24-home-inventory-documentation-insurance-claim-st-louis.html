Home Inventory Guide: Document Your Belongings for Insurance Claims

April 24, 2026

When a house fire, burst pipe, or severe storm hits St. Louis-area homes, most homeowners realize only afterward how much they can't remember. "What did we own?" sounds like an easy question—until it's asked over the ruins of everything you've spent years accumulating. The answer is a thorough home inventory: a documented, up-to-date record of what you own, where it is, and what it's worth. It's the single most powerful tool in your corner when filing an insurance claim in Missouri or Illinois.

Why a Home Inventory Matters for Your Claim

Insurance companies pay based on what you can prove. A detailed home inventory helps you:

  • Avoid missing items: Memory under stress is unreliable. Without records, you'll forget entire categories of belongings—especially in closets, garages, basements, and storage areas.
  • Prove value: Photos, receipts, and serial numbers establish purchase price and replacement cost, reducing disputes with adjusters.
  • Speed up the claim: A prepared inventory lets your adjuster (or your public adjuster) process the claim faster instead of spending weeks reconstructing lists.
  • Coverage verification: The total value helps verify whether your policy's personal property limit is actually sufficient—many St. Louis homeowners are underinsured without realizing it.

How to Create a Home Inventory (Step by Step)

A home inventory doesn't need to be perfect—it just needs to exist, and it needs to be current. Here's the system we recommend:

1. Walk Through Room by Room

Set aside a weekend afternoon and go room-by-room with your phone. Photograph or video every item of value. For electronics, note the brand, model, and serial number. For furniture, estimate the purchase year and approximate cost. Don't skip the attic, garage, basement, or outdoor equipment.

2. Keep Proof of Purchase

Gather receipts, credit card statements, warranties, and appraisals. Store digital copies in a cloud folder (Google Drive, Dropbox, or iCloud) labeled by room or category so they're easy to reference. For high-value items like jewelry, art, or collectibles, get professional appraisals and keep the reports on file.

3. Record Video a Few Times a Year

Record a slow walkthrough video of each room—open drawers, open closets, open cabinets. Narrate as you go: "This is the master bedroom closet—here's my winter coat collection, the Dyson vacuum in the corner, the Pelican case with camera gear." This adds context that still photos alone can't provide. Update this video every 6–12 months.

4. Don't Forget What You Can't See

These categories are the most commonly forgotten:

  • Cleaning supplies and consumables
  • Seasonal decorations (Christmas trees, holiday lighting)
  • Tools in the garage or shed
  • Sporting equipment and outdoor gear
  • Books, media collections
  • Clothing (yes, it adds up—in many claims, clothing alone totals thousands)
  • Pantry contents (groceries lost to refrigerator failure from power outages)

5. Store Your Inventory Off-Site

A paper binder is fine for backup, but store your primary inventory digitally—cloud storage, email attachment to yourself, or a USB drive kept at a friend's or family member's house. If your home is damaged or destroyed, you need access from anywhere.

Special St. Louis Considerations

St. Louis and surrounding Missouri/Illinois communities face unique risks that make a home inventory especially important:

  • Basement flooding: Many homes in flood-prone neighborhoods store expensive items in basements—furnaces, water heaters, home gym equipment, finished room furnishings. These are easily lost in flash floods or sewer backups.
  • Tornado and severe wind events: Missouri averages dozens of tornadoes each year. Whole-room destruction means you'll need to rebuild from scratch—your inventory is the only proof of what existed.
  • Older homes with outdated wiring: Electrical fires remain common in pre-1980s housing stock across St. Louis County. A documented inventory protects your replacement ability after fire damage.
  • Home businesses: If you run a business from home—even a side hustle with professional equipment—keep those purchases documented separately. Standard homeowner policies often exclude or undercover business property.

Free Tools That Help

  • Phone video: The simplest, fastest, and completely free method. Set calendar reminders to update every 6 months.
  • Encircle (by Insurance Institute for Home Business Safety): Free app that guides you through a systematic home inventory.
  • Sortly, Nest Egg, or Memento: Free inventory apps with barcode scanning and cloud sync for tracking items by room or category.
  • Spreadsheet templates: Google Sheets or Excel templates let you track item name, purchase date, price, serial number, and photo links in one place.

What Happens When You Don't Have an Inventory?

Without documentation, you're relying on memory and the adjuster's willingness to trust you. After a major loss, this often results in:

  • Underpayment: Missing items mean missing money. If you can't list 30 items from your garage, that's thousands of dollars left on the table.
  • Delays: Adjusters need specifics to process claims. Vague descriptions cause back-and-forth communications that add weeks to your timeline.
  • Disputes: Without proof, the insurer may dispute the existence or value of high-ticket items, especially when replacement costs are high.

The Bottom Line for St. Louis Homeowners

A home inventory takes a few hours to create—but it takes seconds to lose everything. If you haven't done one yet, start today. Even a single room's worth of photos is better than nothing. Build it room-by-room over a weekend, then schedule a reminder to update it twice a year.
